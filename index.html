Diploma for Labour Migration Experts and Practitioners

A structured learning pathway for strengthening labour migration governance

Turn the labour migration courses you take into one recognised ITCILO qualification. The Diploma gives your learning a clear structure, adds a capstone project built on your own country or institution, and connects you with practitioners worldwide.

One qualification, built from courses you can take one at a time

The Diploma is an advanced, practice-oriented programme for professionals working on labour migration. It supports you in designing, managing and evaluating policies that follow international labour standards and contribute to sustainable development.

Its structure is flexible. You register for individual ITCILO courses, and the Diploma connects the Academy, three eligible courses and a capstone project into a recognised qualification. Your learning adds up, instead of staying scattered across separate short courses.

Throughout, you learn alongside peers from other countries, compare how different systems tackle the same problems, and join an international network of practitioners and experts.

Why a pathway

The need
Labour migration policy now calls for legal knowledge, negotiation skills, data literacy, rights-based approaches and coordination between institutions.
The problem
Learning in this field is often fragmented, and heavy workloads make it hard to plan ahead.
The response
The Diploma adds no extra content. It gives what you already learn more coherence and depth, and it gives that learning recognition.

Who benefits, and how

  • A recognised ITCILO qualification that consolidates several learning investments.
  • A structured pathway in place of fragmented short courses.
  • Comparative country experience and peer learning.
  • Advanced analytical and practical skills you can apply in real contexts.
  • Builds internal expertise on labour migration governance.
  • Supports evidence-based policy design, implementation and evaluation.
  • Contributes to coherence across labour migration, employment, skills and development policy.
  • Strengthens rights-based, fair and effective labour migration governance.
  • Links labour migration to the Sustainable Development Goals, decent work and development outcomes.
  • Anchored in ILO standards and a tripartite approach
  • Focused on practical application and policy impact
  • A global group with diverse institutional perspectives
  • Flexible and cumulative, for professionals in service
  • Access to an international alumni and expert network

Your Diploma pathway

Four requirements make up the Diploma. Select a stop to see what it involves.

Any order, and you can start with any eligible course

All four requirements are completed within five years

Complete the Academy on Labour Migration

The Academy is the one mandatory element. It gives you a shared analytical foundation and policy framework, and anchors the rest of your learning. It does not have to be your first course: you choose how to sequence your learning around your interests and availability.

The 2027 Academy runs from 28 June to 30 July as a blended course, with a face-to-face week in Turin.

View the Academy

Complete three eligible courses

Choose three eligible labour migration courses from the ITCILO portfolio, in any order. They can be online, face-to-face or blended. Each course has its own assessment and leads to an ITCILO Certificate of Achievement, which the Diploma requires for every course you count.

Only courses marked as eligible count. Not every labour migration course in the ITCILO catalogue qualifies.

Explore eligible courses

Complete an individual capstone project

The capstone is a written analytical assignment. You apply what you have learned to a specific country, sector or institution, and develop policy recommendations for it. It turns the knowledge from your courses into practice.

For details on the capstone, contact us.

Receive the Diploma

When you have successfully completed every requirement within five years, you are awarded the Diploma for Labour Migration Experts and Practitioners. You then join the alumni network and can explore what comes next.

You register for courses, not for the Diploma

There is no separate Diploma intake. Pick any eligible course, apply through its own page, and keep going from there.

Every course stays standalone

You can take a course purely for what it teaches. The Diploma is an option, not an obligation.

        Practical information

        How to start

        Register for any one eligible course. You do not apply for “the Diploma”. Courses stay open and standalone, and the Diploma is an option, not a requirement.

        Languages

        All courses are offered in English, and the Academy in English and French. Additional languages may be offered if enough participants ask. Check the language on each course page.

        Registration

        Deadlines and application links are set course by course. Check the course page for the selection criteria and how to apply.

        Institutions

        Institutional nominations and arrangements are possible. Write to migration@itcilo.org to discuss them.

        Frequently asked questions

        Who is the Diploma for?

        It is designed for professionals with experience in labour migration, including government officials and policy planners, workers' and employers' organisations, civil society and migrant-support organisations, international and regional organisations, and researchers, academics and media professionals.

        Participants are expected to have relevant professional experience and an academic background in fields such as public administration, social sciences, labour law or economics.

        How do I start?

        Register for an individual course, either the Academy or any eligible course in the calendar. There is no separate Diploma application or intake. Each course has its own page with dates, deadline and application link.

        Do I have to take the Academy first?

        No. The Academy is mandatory, but it can come at any point in your pathway. Any eligible course can be your starting point, and you choose the sequence that suits your interests and availability.

        What is a Certificate of Achievement, and do I need one for every course?

        Each course has an assessment. When you meet the course requirements you receive an ITCILO Certificate of Achievement. To count towards the Diploma, you need this certificate for each course you complete. Requirements differ from course to course, so check each course page.

        What is the capstone project?

        An individual written analytical assignment in which you apply what you learned to a country, sector or institution you know, and develop policy recommendations. For guidance on format, topic and timing, write to migration@itcilo.org.

        How long do I have to finish?

        All requirements must be completed within five years. The whole Diploma represents about 300 learning hours. If you are unsure how your five years are counted, ask us before you plan.

        What learning formats are available?

        Courses are online, face-to-face or blended. In the 2027 calendar, four courses are online, four are blended with a week in Turin, and one is held face-to-face in Turin. Learning combines self-paced and interactive work, individual and group assignments, and peer-led and tutor-led sessions.

        How do I register for a course?

        Each course has its own page with dates, application deadline and application link. Selection criteria are listed there too. Register separately for every course you want to take.

        Can my organisation nominate several people?

        Yes, institutional nominations and arrangements are possible. Write to migration@itcilo.org to discuss them.

        I took a course in the past. Can it count?

        Write to migration@itcilo.org with the course title, the year and your certificate, and the team will advise. Only courses that qualify for the Diploma, with a Certificate of Achievement, can count.

        Which languages can I study in?

        All courses are offered in English, and the Academy in English and French. Additional languages may be offered if enough people request them. Not every course is available in every language.

        Questions about your pathway?

        The ITCILO labour migration team can help with course choice, institutional arrangements and earlier courses.
